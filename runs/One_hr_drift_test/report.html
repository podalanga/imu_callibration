Findings

  1. Data quality: stillness warn (partly valid, 0.69 % disturbed).
  2. Gyro white noise N = 5.05, 4.92, 4.84 m°/s/√Hz (x, y, z); bias instability B = 15.1, 15.8, 3.1 °/h.
  3. Accelerometer white noise N = 114, 121, 112 µg/√Hz.
  4. Without aiding, after 30 s the heading drifts 0.072° (predicted 0.059°) and the position 7.46 m (predicted 5.00 m), over 45 undisturbed segments.
  5. The largest predicted position term is tilt from gyro (4.98 m at 30 s): better gyro bias estimation (longer still periods, ZARU) is the most effective fix.
  6. Free inertial integration misses the Tier 1 limit of 1 m at 30 s on this phone; aiding is required for Tier 2.
  7. The noise model explains the measured heading drift (measured / predicted = 1.22).
  8. Over 19.0 min, stationary aiding (ZUPT + ZARU) keeps the position error at 0.1 cm, against 75165.8 m for free inertial integration.
  9. Assuming a fixed sample interval instead of hardware timestamps would add only 0.1555° of heading error over the run: timing jitter is negligible.

3. Drift: measured against the noise model

Each segment aligns on 10 s (bias and gravity from the mean), then integrates freely for 60 s: gyro → attitude error θ, accelerometer rotated through θ (a + θ × a, which is where tilt leaks gravity into position) → velocity → position. Measured = RMS over 45 undisturbed segments. Predicted 1σ: heading √((N√t)² + (b t)²) and position √2 · √((N_a t^1.5/√3)² + (b_a t²/2)² + (g b_g t³/6)²), where each bias b = √(B² + N²/t_align) includes the error of the 10 s alignment average.
