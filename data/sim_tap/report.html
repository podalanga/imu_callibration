Findings

  1. Data quality: stillness warn (partly valid, 0.28 % disturbed); duration warn (12.0 min).
  2. Gyro white noise N = 6.54, 6.63, 4.76 m°/s/√Hz (x, y, z); bias instability B = 0.0, 0.0, 0.0 °/h.
  3. Accelerometer white noise N = 137, 119, 193 µg/√Hz.
  4. Without aiding, after 30 s the heading drifts 0.025° (predicted 0.065°) and the position 3.09 m (predicted 2.09 m), over 9 undisturbed segments.
  5. The largest predicted position term is tilt from gyro (2.06 m at 30 s): better gyro bias estimation (longer still periods, ZARU) is the most effective fix.
  6. Free inertial integration misses the Tier 1 limit of 1 m at 30 s on this phone; aiding is required for Tier 2.
  7. The noise model does not fully explain the measured heading drift (measured / predicted = 0.39).
  8. Over 7.0 min, stationary aiding (ZUPT + ZARU) keeps the position error at 0.5 cm, against 632.0 m for free inertial integration.
  9. Assuming a fixed sample interval instead of hardware timestamps would add only 0.0330° of heading error over the run: timing jitter is negligible.

3. Drift: measured against the noise model

Each segment aligns on 10 s (bias and gravity from the mean), then integrates freely for 60 s: gyro → attitude error θ, accelerometer rotated through θ (a + θ × a, which is where tilt leaks gravity into position) → velocity → position. Measured = RMS over 9 undisturbed segments. Predicted 1σ: heading √((N√t)² + (b t)²) and position √2 · √((N_a t^1.5/√3)² + (b_a t²/2)² + (g b_g t³/6)²), where each bias b = √(B² + N²/t_align) includes the error of the 10 s alignment average.
